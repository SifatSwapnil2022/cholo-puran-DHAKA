Old Dhaka, 1608 to today

From Mughal capital to today's old city, with a source for every event.

Open in the 3D guide

Old Dhaka is the city the Mughals made their capital of Bengal in 1610. It lost that role a century later, lived on as a trading town under deputy governors and the East India Company, became the home of the Nawabs of Dhaka, and was the centre of the city until the new town grew north of it in the 20th century. This timeline keeps to the old city and says where each fact comes from. Dates from the 1600s are often given differently by different historians; where they disagree, both are shown.

Before 1608

Before the Mughals মোগলদের আগে

A riverside settlement with a goddess temple that may have given the city its name.

  1. 12th c. The Dhakeshwari Temple is traditionally dated to the Sena king Ballal Sen. The present buildings are much later. See: Dhakeshwari National Temple.
  2. 1602–1604 Raja Man Singh, Akbar's general, camps in Dhaka while fighting the Bara-Bhuiyan chiefs and builds four Shiva temples at the old Dhakeshwari site. See: Dhakeshwari National Temple. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
1608–1717

Capital of Mughal Bengal মোগল বাংলার রাজধানী

Dhaka becomes Jahangirnagar, capital of the Mughal province of Bengal, and grows rich on muslin and river trade.

  1. 1608 Mughal general Islam Khan Chishti defeats Musa Khan, and Dhaka comes under direct Mughal control. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
  2. 1610 Islam Khan moves the capital of Bengal from Rajmahal to Dhaka and renames it Jahangirnagar, after the emperor. People keep calling it Dhaka. Source: Banglapedia: Dhaka; Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
  3. 1640 The Portuguese traveller Sebastien Manrique puts the population of Dhaka and its suburbs at about 200,000. Source: Banglapedia: Dhaka.
  4. 1644–1646 Mir Abul Qasim, diwan of Prince Shah Shuja, builds the Bara Katra, a great travellers' inn facing the river. See: Bara Katra. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka; Banglapedia: Dhaka.
  5. 1660 Mir Jumla arrives as governor and Dhaka is the capital again. His roads and gate link the city to forts against the Magh pirates. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka; Banglapedia: Dhaka.
  6. 1663–1671 Subahdar Shaista Khan builds the Choto Katra. See: Choto Katra.
  7. 1663–1682 The Dutch (1663), English (1667–68) and French (1682) open trading factories. The French site later became Ahsan Manzil. See: Ahsan Manzil. Source: Banglapedia: Dhaka; Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
  8. 1664–1688 Shaista Khan's governorship is the city's biggest building boom. The Chawk Mosque by the market square dates from 1676. See: Chawkbazar Shahi Mosque. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
  9. 1678 Prince Muhammad Azam, son of Aurangzeb, begins the fort at Lalbagh. He is recalled before it is finished. See: Lalbagh Fort. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka; Banglapedia: Dhaka.
  10. 1684 Shaista Khan's daughter Pari Bibi dies and work on Lalbagh Fort stops. Her tomb stands at its centre. See: Lalbagh Fort.
  11. 16th–18th c. Dhaka is the world centre of muslin, a cotton cloth so fine that the finest grades were reserved for the Mughal court. Source: Wikipedia: Dhaka muslin.
  12. 1700–1706 Kartalab Khan (Murshid Quli Khan, then Dhaka's diwan) builds the Begum Bazar mosque, and Khan Mohammad Mridha raises his mosque on a high platform. See: Kartalab Khan Mosque, Khan Mohammad Mridha Mosque.
  13. 1717 The capital of Bengal moves to Murshidabad, and Dhaka begins a long decline. Source: Banglapedia: Dhaka; Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
1717–1857

Deputy governors and the Company নায়েব নাজিম ও কোম্পানি

Dhaka is ruled by deputy governors (Naib Nazims), then by the East India Company. Armenian and European merchants settle, and the muslin trade collapses.

  1. 1728 Naib Nazim Mirza Lutfullah builds the Chowk Bazaar, still the old city's market square. See: Chawkbazar iftar market. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
  2. c. 1760 Rice-husking workers settle in Dhaka. Their speech becomes Dhakaiya Kutti, the old city's own Bangla. Source: Wikipedia: Dhakaiya Kutti dialect.
  3. 1765–1766 The East India Company takes over Bengal's revenue. The Naib Nazims move into a new palace at Nimtali; its gateway survives. See: Nimtali Deuri (Asiatic Society Heritage Museum). Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka; Banglapedia: Dhaka.
  4. 1781 The Armenian community builds the Church of the Holy Resurrection in Armanitola. See: Armenian Church of the Holy Resurrection. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
  5. 1793 The Company abolishes the Nizamat and takes full control. The city is now written "Dacca". Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
  6. c. 1800 British observers already call Dhaka muslin extinct: British tariffs and cheap Lancashire cotton have ruined the weavers. Source: Wikipedia: Dhaka muslin.
  7. 1801–1840 The population falls from about 200,000 to 51,636. Source: Banglapedia: Dhaka.
  8. 1819–1824 St Thomas Church is built near the river and consecrated. See: St Thomas Church.
  9. 1825 Armenian merchant Stephen Aratoon builds the mansion that becomes Ruplal House. See: Ruplal House.
  10. 1835 The Dhaka English Seminary opens; it is now Dhaka Collegiate School. See: Dhaka Collegiate School.
  11. 1843 The last Naib Nazim dies and the office ends. The head of the Khwaja family estate becomes known as the Nawab of Dhaka. See: Nimtali Deuri (Asiatic Society Heritage Museum). Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka; Banglapedia: Dhaka.
  12. 1848 Nicholas Pogose, an Armenian, founds Pogose School, the country's first private school. See: Pogose School.
1857–1947

The Nawabs and the Raj নবাব ও ব্রিটিশ রাজ

The Nawabs build Ahsan Manzil, the city gets a municipality, a railway and a riverfront promenade, and in 1905 Dhaka briefly becomes a provincial capital.

  1. 1857 During the sepoy uprising, British troops and sepoys clash at Lalbagh Fort on 22 November. Captured sepoys are hanged at Antaghar, today's Bahadur Shah Park. See: Lalbagh Fort, Bahadur Shah Park. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
  2. 1858 The Dhaka Brahma School opens; it grows into Jagannath University. See: Jagannath University.
  3. 1859–1872 Nawab Abdul Ghani builds Ahsan Manzil on the river. See: Ahsan Manzil.
  4. 1864 The Dacca Municipality is founded, and the Buckland Bund promenade is built along the Sadarghat waterfront. See: Sadarghat Launch Terminal. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka; Banglapedia: Dhaka.
  5. 1872 The first census counts 69,212 people. Source: Banglapedia: Dhaka.
  6. 1880 Northbrook Hall opens as the town hall. See: Northbrook Hall (Lalkuthi).
  7. 1885 The railway reaches Dhaka from Narayanganj, cutting through the Nimtali–Fulbaria area. Source: Banglapedia: Dhaka; Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
  8. 1888 A tornado wrecks Ahsan Manzil except its Rangmahal. See: Ahsan Manzil.
  9. 1897 A great earthquake damages Ahsan Manzil, the Armenian Church tower, Ruplal House and Hussaini Dalan. See: Ahsan Manzil, Armenian Church of the Holy Resurrection, Ruplal House, Hussaini Dalan.
  10. 1905–1911 Dhaka becomes capital of Eastern Bengal and Assam; the partition is annulled in 1911. The Nawab of Dhaka, Salimullah, helps found the Muslim League in Dhaka in 1906. See: Ahsan Manzil. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka; Banglapedia: Dhaka.
  11. 1914 Jogesh Chandra Ghosh founds Sadhana Aushadhalaya, an ayurvedic factory in Gendaria. See: Sadhana Aushadhalaya.
  12. 1922–1939 Beauty Lassi (1922) and Haji Biryani (1939) open, two of the old city's longest-running food shops. See: Beauty Lassi, Haji Biryani.
  13. 1947 Partition. Dhaka becomes capital of East Bengal in Pakistan. Some Hindu families leave, among them the owners of the Sutrapur Zamindar Bari, and Dhakeshwari's original idol goes to Kolkata. See: Dhakeshwari National Temple, Sutrapur Zamindar Bari. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
1947–1971

The Pakistan years and 1971 পাকিস্তান আমল ও ১৯৭১

Old Dhaka becomes the base of East Pakistan's publishers and the birthplace of the Awami League, and is attacked in 1971.

  1. 1949 The Awami Muslim League, later the Awami League, is founded at Rose Garden Palace on 23 June. See: Rose Garden Palace.
  2. c. 1951 Beauty Boarding opens and becomes a meeting place for writers and artists. See: Beauty Boarding.
  3. 1954 The Eid procession, an old Dhaka tradition, is stopped by the district magistrate. It does not return for 42 years. Source: The Daily Star: Historical glimpses of Eid processions in Dhaka (Mar 2026).
  4. 1957 Victoria Park is renamed Bahadur Shah Park, after the last Mughal emperor. See: Bahadur Shah Park.
  5. 26 Mar 1971 The Pakistan army attacks Shankhari Bazar, killing Hindu residents and burning houses. Survivors flee across the river. A memorial stands at the east end of the road. See: Shankhari Bazar. Source: Wikipedia: Shankharibazar massacre.
  6. 1971 Jogesh Chandra Ghosh, founder of Sadhana Aushadhalaya, is killed during the war. Dhaka is liberated on 16 December and becomes the capital of Bangladesh. See: Sadhana Aushadhalaya. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka; Banglapedia: Dhaka.
1971–2026

Bangladesh বাংলাদেশ

The old city restores its palaces, loses some of its heritage, lives through deadly fires, and gets its crafts and festivals recognised again.

  1. 1985–1992 Ahsan Manzil is restored and opens as a branch of the National Museum. See: Ahsan Manzil.
  2. 1994 The Eid procession is revived, with young people from about 30 neighbourhoods. Source: The Daily Star: Historical glimpses of Eid processions in Dhaka (Mar 2026).
  3. 1996 Dhakeshwari is declared the National Temple. See: Dhakeshwari National Temple.
  4. 2005 Jagannath College becomes Jagannath University. See: Jagannath University.
  5. 2009–2011 The Asiatic Society restores the Nimtali gateway. See: Nimtali Deuri (Asiatic Society Heritage Museum).
  6. 3 Jun 2010 A fire spreading from a chemical store in Nimtali kills at least 124 people. Source: TBS: Deadly fires in old Dhaka (Aug 2022).
  7. 2011 Dhaka City Corporation splits in two; Old Dhaka falls under Dhaka South. Source: Wikipedia: History of Dhaka.
  8. 2014 The government launches a project to bring back Dhaka muslin. Source: Wikipedia: Dhaka muslin.
  9. 20 Feb 2019 A chemical vapour explosion at Churihatta, Chawkbazar, starts a fire that kills 71 people. See: Chawkbazar iftar market. Source: TBS: Deadly fires in old Dhaka (Aug 2022).
  10. 2022 Part of the Bara Katra is demolished amid ownership disputes. The Padma Bridge opens, and the Sadarghat launch fleet starts to shrink. See: Bara Katra, Sadarghat Launch Terminal.
  11. 2023 UNESCO lists Dhaka's rickshaws and rickshaw painting as intangible cultural heritage. See: Bangshal Road rickshaw market. Source: UNESCO: Rickshaws and rickshaw painting in Dhaka (inscribed 2023).
  12. 2025 Dhaka's city corporation stages a large Eid procession again. Source: TBS: Dhaka celebrates Eid with a Mughal-style procession (Mar 2025); The Daily Star: Historical glimpses of Eid processions in Dhaka (Mar 2026).
  13. 2026 Restoration of Northbrook Hall is largely complete by May. See: Northbrook Hall (Lalkuthi).
