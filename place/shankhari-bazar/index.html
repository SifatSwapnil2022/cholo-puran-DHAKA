Shankhari Bazar

The conch-bangle lane

Why go

A narrow street of 12-foot-wide, very deep houses, small shrines, and shops cutting conch-shell bangles. During Durga Puja it is lit up end to end; on normal days it is quieter and worn.

Festivals here

  • Durga Puja, Five days in late September or October. Dhaka's biggest Durga Puja is at Dhakeshwari, and Shankhari Bazar is lit up end to end. It ends with processions taking the idols to the river.
  • Shakrain, 14 January, the last day of Poush. Families fly and fight kites from the rooftops of Shankhari Bazar, Sutrapur, Gendaria, Narinda and Lakshmibazar, and make pitha at home. After dark it turns into fireworks, lanterns and loud music.
  • Kali Puja and Diwali, The new-moon night of Kartik, in October or November. Around a hundred pandals hang over the narrow lanes of Shankhari Bazar and Tanti Bazar, and you walk underneath them to see the goddess. Families light clay lamps at home, at temples and at crematoriums to remember their elders. The pandals stay up for five or six days before the immersion.
  • Saraswati Puja, Vasant Panchami, in late January or February. The goddess of learning, and above all a students' festival. In Old Dhaka the puja is held in Shankhari Bazar, Tanti Bazar and at Jagannath University, with pandals in schools, temples and lanes.
  • Holi (Dol Purnima), The full moon of Falgun, in March. Shankhari Bazar turns into a street party: powder colours, water guns, buckets from the balconies and loud music. It is Hindu in origin, but people of every faith join in and plenty come from outside Old Dhaka to take part.

Crafts to see

  • Conch-shell bangles. Watch the cutters at work in the tiny shops of Shankhari Bazar. The shells come from Sri Lanka.

History

Named after the Shankhari community of conch-shell artisans. The 1971 massacre here devastated the community. Houses are about 12 ft wide and 70–100 ft deep, each traditionally with a shrine room.

Photos

Look straight down the lane to compress the narrow façades; at Puja, the lights after dark. Inside: Ask before photographing inside homes or shrines.
